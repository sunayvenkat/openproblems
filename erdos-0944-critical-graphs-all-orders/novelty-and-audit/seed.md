# Kohlmeyer–Kruer seed theorem: adversarial source audit

## What source and theorem were actually checked?

### Takeaway

I found no counterexample or fatal gap in the displayed ordinary-mathematics proof of the finite (4,r)-seed theorem. I checked every stage of Sections 2–7, including the counting arguments that survive edge deduplication and the exact character/rounding argument. This is an independent mathematical audit with finite supplementary checks, not an independent Lean rebuild or a blanket certification of all 570 claimed formal theorems.

### Cited Findings

- The checked source is **Liam Kruer and Jensen Kohlmeyer, _Vertex-critical four-chromatic graphs with no small critical edge set_**, manuscript dated September 10, 2026, 13 pages. GitHub's live history reported the latest PDF change at commit `8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23`, authored October 10, 2026. ## Does the graph criterion really imply a vertex-critical, edge-robust four-chromatic graph?

### Takeaway

The criterion's combinatorial and height arguments survive the intended adversarial attacks. Deduplicating chords does not invalidate the count of bad core directions; no unproved global edge-disjointness of translated witness paths is used. Vertex deletion is handled explicitly for every vertex, without assuming the augmented graph is vertex-transitive.

### Cited Findings

The criterion takes n≡1 mod 3, t≥r≥1, q=3t+1 dividing n with q<n, q units a_i of distinct unordered direction pairs, and an inverse-closed nonzero S containing their signs but no involution. It defines

`N_n={1,n−1} ∪ {0<s<n : s≡2 (mod 3)}`,

`J={s∈S: 0<s<n/2}`,

`D_K(s)=max(0,n−3 min([Ks]_n,n−[Ks]_n))`, and `F(K)=Σ_{s∈J}D_K(s)`.

Its hypotheses are (i) S⊆a_i N_n for every i; (ii) off-diagonal normalized chord lengths ℓa_i/a_j, for ℓ=4,7,…,q, have residue 2 mod 3; (iii) each s∈J has r three-step words summing to s, each avoiding its target pair, with pair-sets disjoint between words; (iv) F(K)≤r forces a positive deficit at some a_i. The graph adds each chord `{x+j a_i, x+(j+ℓ)a_i}` for x≡i mod q and 0≤j≤q−ℓ. [Manuscript, p. 2, Theorem 2.1](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

### Inferences

The following are my mathematical checks of that cited criterion and its proof.

**Simplicity, loops, and collisions.** A core edge has nonzero difference. A chord difference is ℓa_i with 4≤ℓ≤q<n, hence nonzero because a_i is a unit. Each q-step gap has q+1 distinct vertices since q<n. Chord duplicates are deliberately identified; they cannot create loops. A core undirected edge uniquely determines its unordered difference pair `{±(v−u)}`. Thus disjointness of core-edge sets for distinct direction pairs is unaffected by chords that coincide with each other or with a core edge. The augmented graph need only **contain** the required B_t subgraph on each gap; additional edges do not hurt the argument. [Manuscript, pp. 2–4](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Every deleted vertex.** For arbitrary v set i=[v]_q and color u≠v by `[[a_i^{-1}(u−v)]_n]_3`. A difference 2 mod 3 changes color by 2 without a wrap and by 1 with a wrap; normalized differences ±1 are proper after coordinate zero is removed. For a chord of type i, a_i is also a unit modulo q, so consecutive vertices in residue class i along its Hamilton cycle occur exactly q steps apart. The entire chord lies within one such gap. No v≡i mod q is strictly inside its forward arc; an endpoint v deletes the edge. Otherwise the normalized chord has no wrap and its length is 1 mod 3. Chords of every other type are proper by (ii). This works for every v; no symmetry of the augmented graph is asserted. Assigning v a fourth color gives χ(G)≤4. [Manuscript, §2.1](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Untouched unit after at most r bad edges.** In any 3-coloring with at most r distinct monochromatic graph edges, call a core direction pair bad if any edge of that pair is monochromatic. Every bad pair consumes a different core edge, so at most r pairs are bad. The q selected unit pairs are distinct and q=3t+1>r; at least one is wholly proper. Consequently the wholly proper directions still generate Z/nZ. An edge that is also represented as many chords still spoils only its one core direction pair. [Manuscript, §2.3; deduction spelled out here](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Height character.** Pull the proper coloring back from Z/nZ to the integer grid with one coordinate per generator pair. A proper oriented edge has increment ±1 congruent to the color difference mod 3. A grid square has an even total divisible by 3, between −4 and 4; only zero is possible. Backtracks cancel, and grid closed walks are generated by squares and backtracks, so an integer height exists. If λ is in the kernel lattice, the period difference is color-divisible by 3, and moving its basepoint changes it by an element of {−2,0,2} divisible by 3; hence it is constant. The period is an additive functional θ on the lattice. Lattice finite index permits its real-linear extension. From a period n e_j, |θ_j|≤1. Height parity gives `θ·λ ≡ Σλ_j (mod 2)` and color gives `θ·λ≡0 (mod 3)`, so `(θ−3·1)/6` is integer-valued on the lattice. Its reduction mod integers is a character of Z/nZ, hence `θ_u ≡ 3+6Ku/n (mod 6)` for a single K. Negative steps contribute −3≡3 mod 6, so the same congruence holds for every odd word. Translation averaging of a word is the signed sum of the generator means; period constants cancel when changing lifts of its initial vertex. [Manuscript, Lemma 2.2](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Witness-word robustness and the undirected count.** If target s is bad, its own pair already consumes one of at most r bad pairs. The r words in (iii) avoid that pair, and their direction-pair sets are disjoint between words. Therefore at most r−1 other bad pairs can spoil at most r−1 words. A remaining word uses wholly proper directions at every translate. A proper target instead uses (s,s,−s). No assertion that all translated paths are edge-disjoint is needed; paths may repeat vertices or steps. A three-step increment δ_s(v) lies in {−3,−1,1,3}. It equals ±1 at a proper target edge and ±3 at a monochromatic target edge. For a non-involution s, the map v↦{v,v+s} is injective: a collision with swapped endpoints would force 2s=0. Thus a direction pair has exactly n distinct edges, and with β_s bad ones, `|mean δ_s|≤1+2β_s/n`. The least absolute value allowed by its character congruence is `3−6 min([Ks]_n/n,1−[Ks]_n/n)`. Hence β_s≥D_K(s). Distinct J-pairs have disjoint core-edge sets, so `F(K)≤Σβ_s≤r`. This gives the needed condition-(iv) unit with mean magnitude greater than 1. [Manuscript, §2.3, equations (7)–(8)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Winding lemma.** B_t has 3t+2 cyclically arranged vertices and edges at forward linear differences 1 mod 3. It is rotation-invariant because reversing a cyclic difference replaces d by (3t+2)−d≡1 when d≡1. If b≤t cycle edges are bad, the 3t+2−b proper edges exceed 2b; therefore a bad edge is followed by at least three proper ones. Deleting their three interior vertices preserves the induced B_{t−1} pattern (all changed differences decrease by 3) and removes at least the initial bad edge. If the three proper increments total s∈{−3,−1,1,3}, the new bridge's maximal increment is respectively 3,−1,1,3. The old maximal cycle total minus the new one is −3,3,3,3, always at most 3. The no-bad-cycle case follows from the total's parity and divisibility by 3; t=0 is a proper edge traversed both ways. Thus maximal cycle total≤3t; since the closing increment≥−1, path total≤3t+1. Reversal gives the lower bound. [Manuscript, Lemma 2.3, p. 4](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Closing the contradiction and exact criticality.** Choose orientation so the exceptional unit's mean exceeds 1. Its n translated increments total more than n. The n/q designated gaps partition its Hamilton-cycle edges, so a gap totals more than q=3t+1. Its B_t copy has at most r≤t bad edges, contradicting the winding lemma. Reversing orientation preserves the same gap vertex sets: a gap's other endpoint is still in residue class i because q divides n. Hence every 3-coloring has more than r bad edges. This excludes a 3-coloring of G and any 3-coloring after deleting at most r edges. Finally if some G−v were 2-colorable, giving v a third color would color G with three colors, already excluded. Thus all vertex-deleted chromatic numbers are exactly 3. [Manuscript, proof of Theorem 2.1, p. 4](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

### Gaps

No unresolved mathematical gap was identified in this criterion. The formalization of these claims was not rebuilt. Numerical winding checks below are supplementary, not a substitute for the above induction.

## Do the CRT parameters, exact rigidity and word construction actually satisfy the criterion?

### Takeaway

The stated parameter bounds suffice for every displayed inequality and construction I checked. The five-block character argument does not silently assume small coefficients; it derives sparsity from the width of an independent CRT product. The finite rounding argument avoids the removed involution and forces exact, rather than approximate, middle-third membership.

### Cited Findings

The source sets t=r unless 5 divides 3r+1, in which case t=r+1; q=3t+1; D=30q; M=5D. It chooses κ_j≡0 mod 3 and κ_j≡1 mod (M−5), with d_j=D(κ_j−1)+1, a_j=5d_j, κ_j≥6, a_j>10q a_{j−1}, and d_j≡κ_i^{-1} mod d_i for i<j; a_0=1. Set `L=15∏d_j`. The order satisfies n≡1 mod 3, n≡0 mod q, n≡−κ_j^{-1} mod a_j, and `n>1000(r+1)qL²`. [Manuscript, §3, equations (9)–(19)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

### Inferences

**CRT existence and parameter inequalities.** If 5 divides 3r+1, replacing r by r+1 changes q by 3, so the chosen q is coprime to 15 and t≥r. Each d_i is 1 mod D and 1 mod (D−1); also d_i≡1 mod 5, giving coprimality with `M−5=5(D−1)`. A common divisor of κ_i and d_i divides D−1, but κ_i≡1 mod D−1; so κ_i is invertible mod d_i and mod a_i. The next congruence in κ_j has invertible coefficient D modulo each d_i. The moduli 3, M−5 and prior d_i are pairwise coprime, with d_j≡κ_i^{-1} guaranteeing the pairwise induction. CRT gives an unbounded progression, so the size conditions may be imposed without conflict. For n, all prescribed residues modulo a_j agree at their shared factor 5, namely −1. Separate 5 from the pairwise coprime d_j and use CRT for 3,q,5,d_1,…; q is coprime to every d_j because d_j≡1 mod q. The progression step is exactly qL. Every a_j is a unit mod n. Also a_j≤L/3 and the lower bound on n makes every a_j<n/2, every d_j>8 and every a_j>100. These are actual consequences of the stated choices, not extra hypotheses. [Audit of manuscript, Lemma 3.1 and equations (16)–(20)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Short ratios and the core.** The inverse is `(1+κ_j n)/a_j`; it is an integer in (0,n). For i≠j put ℓ=[a_iκ_j]_{a_j}; then `R_ij=(a_i+ℓn)/a_j`. If i=0, ℓ=κ_j and ℓ/a_j<1/(4q), using κ_j≥6 and D=30q. If i>j≥1, ℓ=5 by the recursive congruence. If 0<i<j, the exact identity is `a_iκ_j/a_j=(κ_i−1)+5/M+(a_i/a_j)(1−5/M)`. Its fractional part lies in `(0,1/(30q)+1/(10q))⊂(0,1/(4q))`; no wrap is hidden. Its integer part is κ_i−1, which implies ℓ≡2 mod 3. For i=0, ℓ≡0. Together with a_i≡a_j≡2 mod 3 for positive indices and n≡1, these give R_ij≡2 mod 3. The contribution a_i/(a_j n) is <1/(4q), so R_ij<n/(2q), certainly n/q. When j=0 the assertion follows directly from a_i<n/q. Ratios cannot be 1 or n−1, giving distinct unit direction pairs. Multiplying any off-diagonal ratio by ℓ∈{4,7,…,q} does not wrap and preserves residue 2. Thus `S=(∩a_i N_n) \ {n/2 if n even}` is inverse-closed, nonzero, involution-free, contains every ±a_i and verifies (i)–(ii). [Audit of manuscript, Lemma 3.2 and §4](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Generic CRT classes.** For 0<s<n, with e_j(s)=[κ_js]_{a_j}, the exact least normalized residue is `(s+e_j(s)n)/a_j`: divisibility follows from κ_j n≡−1 mod a_j; its bounds follow from 0<s<n and 0≤e_j<a_j. Away from ±a_i, membership in S is exactly s≡2 mod 3 and e_j(s)≡2 mod 3 for every j, apart from the removed involution. This condition has period L. Since κ_j≡1 mod 5, e_j(s) and s have the same mod-5 residue. Its five product blocks use `e_j=2+3h+15y_j`, h=0,…,4. With m_j=(d_j−1)/3, the upper bound is m_j for h=0 and m_j−1 for h>0: this follows by listing residues below 5d_j. Each coordinate is independent mod d_j by CRT, as gcd(15κ_j,d_j)=1. All blocks are nonempty, so there is more than one generic residue class. [Audit of manuscript, §4, equations (25)–(28)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Exact periodic rigidity, including coefficient sparsity.** Assume all `αρ+βx mod 1`, for generic classes ρ and 0≤x≤1, lie in B=[1/3,2/3], where α∈L^{-1}Z and K=αn+β∈Z. A continuous path cannot wrap around the circle while staying in B; hence |β|≤1/3, and all initial phases have representatives in one interval I⊆B of width at most 1/3−|β|. Decompose the character into independent mod-3, mod-5 and mod-d_j coordinates. On the h=0 block, the actual phase change on increasing y_j by one lies in [−1/3,1/3] and has a fixed residue mod 1. There is at most one such representative, because that interval's length is 2/3<1. Thus changes are constant, `δ_j=z_j/d_j`, throughout the product, and the total range is `Σ m_j|δ_j|≤1/3`. Since m_j=(d_j−1)/3 and d_j>8, any nonzero z_j must be ±1, and two active coordinates would already give more than 1/3. No boundedness of the original character coefficients is assumed. [Audit of manuscript, Lemma 5.1, pp. 7–8](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

With no active coordinate, all mod-d_j coefficients vanish. A nonzero mod-5 coefficient produces all five equally spaced phases and cannot fit in I. The remaining frequency is α≡1/3 or 2/3; integrality and n≡1 mod 3 force β=−1/3 or +1/3, respectively. Hence K≡±(n−1)/3 mod n, using a_0=1. [Audit of manuscript, Lemma 5.1, no-active case](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

With one active coordinate, negate the character if necessary so δ=1/d, and choose coefficient c=(1−d)/15. The five block first phases differ by `(3v−1)/5+1/(5d)` mod 1. Their real representatives all lie in an interval of width at most `4/(3d)`, because each block has width at least `(m−1)/d`. For v≠2 mod 5 the distance of this phase difference to an integer is at least `(d−1)/(5d)>4/(3d)` exactly when d>23/3; d>8 suffices. Therefore v=2. The first phases advance by 1/(5d), and the h=0 block's interval contains all the other block intervals: their starts move by at most 4/(5d), while their widths are shorter by 1/d. Its location in B forces u=1 mod 3 and its phase range is `[1/3+2/(3a), 2/3−1/a]`, a=5d. Consequently `−2/(3a)≤β≤1/a`. The frequency simplifies to `α≡1/3+κ/(3a)` using κ≡6 mod 15. The value `((a+κ)n+1)/(3a)` is integral: the numerator is divisible by a through κn≡−1 and divisible by 3 through a≡2, κ≡0, n≡1. The allowable β interval has length 5/(3a)<1 and contains 1/(3a), leaving that unique value by integrality. Hence 3Ka≡1 mod n and, since 3 is invertible mod n, `Ka≡(2n+1)/3=−(n−1)/3 mod n`. Negation supplies the opposite sign. [Audit of manuscript, equations (33)–(37)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Finite rounding; the involution and endpoints.** F(K)≤r implies every core phase lies in `B_ε=[1/3−ε,2/3+ε]`, ε=r/(3n), of width w=1/3+2ε<1/2. At most one generic class contains n/2, while there is more than one class; choose another class. Its positive representatives below n form an uninterrupted progression of step L, with at least n/L−1 points. Consecutive phase differences belong to [−w,w], whose length 2w<1, so they are all the same real representative δ of KL/n modulo 1. Telescoping gives `|δ|(n/L−2)≤w`. Write δ=KL/n−p, α=p/L, β=nδ/L; then K=αn+β and `|β|≤w/(1−2L/n)<1/2`. The latter strict inequality follows, for example, from `n>4r+6L`, much weaker than the stated n bound. [Audit of manuscript, Proposition 6.1, equations (38)–(39)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

For **every** generic class, its first and last representatives are within L of 0 and n; neither can be the removed midpoint since n>2L. Their phases approximate αρ and αρ+β to within `ε+|β|L/n < (r/3+L/2)/n <1/L`. Both target phases are exact L^{-1}-grid points because α=p/L and β=K−pn/L. Both endpoints of B lie on the same grid. A grid point outside B is at circular distance at least 1/L, forcing both target phases into B exactly. Their B-representatives differ by at most 1/3; their difference is β modulo 1, and |β|<1/2 rules out an added nonzero integer (which would have magnitude at least 2/3). Hence the whole straight segment stays in B, fulfilling Lemma 5.1, not merely its endpoints modulo 1. Its canonical unit value ±(n−1)/3 has deficit n−3((n−1)/3)=1. This proves condition (iv). [Audit of manuscript, Proposition 6.1, equation (40)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

**Three-step words, ranges and forbidden signs.** For z∈J, either z=1 or z≡2 mod 3. A nongeneric positive orientation is some a_j, since every −a_j exceeds n/2. Set W=z+2n for z=1 and W=z+n otherwise. Then n<W≤2n+1 and W≡0 mod 3. For each j set M_j to a_j+κ_j−2, 2a_j−1, or a_j+e_j(z)−1 in the respective cases z=1, z=a_j, and all others. In the last case e_j(z)≡2 mod 3 even if z is another unit: this follows from the exact normalization and short compatibility. In all cases a_j<M_j<2a_j, M_j≡0 mod 3 and M_j≡κ_jW mod a_j; their common mod-5 residue is z−c, where c=2 or 1. The formula `E_j=2+15 floor((M_j/3−2)/15)` gives integer b_j=M_j/3−E_j in [0,14]; the three coordinates E_j,E_j,M_j−2E_j are positive and below a_j because a_j>100>84. All are 2 mod 3, and their mod-5 residues 2,2,z−c−4 are independent of j. CRT gives three generic classes whose sum is W mod L. [Audit of manuscript, §7, equations (41)–(45)](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/8b9db1d8c0ded5398d5e4e4bbdf8f2ec14ce5c23/papers/erdos-944.pdf).

Choose s_1,s_2 in their classes within W/3±n/24 and set s_3=W−s_1−s_2; s_3 lies within W/3±n/12. For n<W≤2n+1 these intervals lie strictly within (0,n), using n>2. Each first-two progression offers at least n/(12L)−1 points. After adding the possible involution to a forbidden F of size≤6r+2, at most 6r+3 values of s_1 and at most 2(6r+3) values of s_2 are excluded; the latter bound accounts separately for s_2 and s_3. The stated n bound implies `n/(12L)−1>2(6r+3)` by a very large margin. Hence a valid word exists for every such F. Start by forbidding ±z; each chosen word adds at most six forbidden signed directions. Before the r-th selection the count is≤2+6(r−1), which is within the lemma's allowance. This gives the exact pair-disjointness required in (iii). Internal repetitions in a word do not hurt and need not be excluded.
