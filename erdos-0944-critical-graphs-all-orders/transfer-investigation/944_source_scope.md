# Erdős 944 source scope and attribution

## What is the exact remaining assertion?

### Takeaway

The stronger all-orders form is a genuine separately stated question: if f_k(n) is the largest edge-deletion tolerance of a k-vertex-critical n-vertex graph, does f_k(n) tend to infinity as n tends to infinity? The Kohlmeyer–Kruer manuscript explicitly supplies existence at some order for each tolerance but declines to claim every sufficiently large order.

### Cited Findings

- Skottova–Steiner Problem 1.1 defines f_k(n) as the largest r>=1 for which a (k,r)-graph of order n exists, setting f_k(n)=0 if none exists. It asks whether f_k(n) tends to infinity. Here a (k,r)-graph is k-vertex-critical and remains k-chromatic after deletion of any set of at most r edges. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)
- Their Theorem 1.2 establishes f_k(n)=Omega(n^(1/3)) for every fixed k>=5. Their results leave k=4 open. Theorem 1.4 gives a sublinear upper bound for all k>=4. [Primary paper, introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)
- The Erdős Problems record likewise distinguishes arbitrary-tolerance existence from the limit over all orders. [Problem history and statement](https://www.erdosproblems.com/history/944)
- Kruer and Kohlmeyer's Theorem 1.1 supplies a finite (4,r)-graph for every r>=1. The first page explicitly says that the manuscript does not claim graphs of every sufficiently large order. [Authors' primary manuscript](https://github.com/JENW1N/complete-math-solutions/blob/main/papers/erdos-944.pdf)

### Inferences

A valid argument extending one (4,r)-seed to all sufficiently large orders would close this specific scope gap. Applying it separately for each r would give f_4(n)->infinity. No new construction of the seed itself would be required.

### Gaps

The current source survey did not find a later published all-orders four-colour result, but this is bounded search evidence, not a proof of priority.

## Which construction lemma must receive prior credit?

### Takeaway

The split-vertex substitution into a critical skeleton is already precisely Skottova–Steiner Lemma 2.1. The manuscript must cite it, even if it re-proves the lemma for self-contained exposition.

### Cited Findings

- Lemma 2.1 applies to every k>=4 and r>=1. If (k,r)-graphs have orders n_1,...,n_t and a k-critical graph H has order h and t edges, there is a (k,r)-graph of order h+sum_i(n_i-1). [Primary paper, Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)
- Its proof deletes a vertex v_i from each seed, partitions the neighbours according to one colour versus the other k-2 colours in a proper (k-1)-colouring, and attaches the two parts to the endpoints of an edge of H while deleting that skeleton edge. This is exactly the split-vertex inequality gadget contemplated in the current investigation. The proof checks robustness by identifying equally coloured terminals back to v_i, and vertex-criticality by considering deletion inside a gadget or at a skeleton vertex. [Primary paper, Lemma 2.1 proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)
- The paper credits the gluing proof idea as partly inspired by earlier work of Wang. [Primary paper, beginning of Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)

### Inferences

With all seeds equal to one q-vertex graph, the available orders are h+(q-1)e(H). Thus the remaining task is arithmetic control of (|V(H)|,|E(H)|) for 4-critical skeletons, while preserving their criticality.

### Gaps

No novelty claim should be attached to the gadget, its vertex-deletion proof, or its robustness proof. Those are already published in Lemma 2.1.

## Does the published paper already prove the single-seed order completion?

### Takeaway

The inspected paper proves order completion from a whole arithmetic progression of seed orders, not from a single arbitrary seed. Its stated theorem does not cover k=4. The proposed use of a numerical semigroup generated by several critical skeletons is not stated in that paper.

### Cited Findings

- Lemma 2.2 gives a k-critical graph of every order n>=k+3 with at most (k-2)n edges. For k=4 it uses odd wheels for even n, and a Hajós join with K4 for odd n. It explicitly records that a Hajós join preserves k-criticality. [Primary paper, Lemma 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)
- Theorem 2.3 assumes k>=5 and uses the full arithmetic progression of seed orders from Theorem 1.3. Its proof selects a skeleton order h in a prescribed residue class, then distributes n-h among the t seed orders. It does not derive all-orders existence from one unrestricted seed. [Primary paper, Theorem 2.3 proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)
- Section 5 still describes the case k=4 as open. No Grötzsch-graph or gcd/numerical-semigroup argument appears in the inspected text. [Primary paper, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08703)

### Inferences

For the parent's proposed skeletons K4, W5 (five-cycle plus hub), and the Grötzsch graph, their (vertex,edge) pairs are (4,6), (6,10), and (11,20). If c=q-1, starting with K4 and taking Hajós joins with copies of these graphs gives substituted orders

N=4+6c+a(3+5c)+b(5+9c)+d(10+19c).

The arithmetic identity 9(3+5c)-5(5+9c)=2 shows that the first two generators have gcd either one or two. If c is even, both are odd and their gcd is one. If c is odd, both are even while 10+19c is odd; the three-generator gcd is therefore one in every case. Consequently their nonnegative integer span contains every sufficiently large integer. This is the plausible order-completion step; the other agents are independently checking criticality of the chosen skeletons and the full composed construction.

The appropriate attribution is: Kohlmeyer–Kruer's seed theorem, Skottova–Steiner's published gluing lemma, and an elementary arithmetic completion argument supplied in this investigation. Even if correct, the latter's independent novelty remains uncertified.
