\noindent\textbf{Task.} Let $p$ be prime, $M_p=2^p-1$, and $P_K=\{p:\tau(2^p+1)\le K\}$. Set
\[
\beta_K=\limsup_{p\in P_K}\frac{\log\omega(M_p)\,\log\log p}{\log p}\qquad(\beta_K:=0\text{ if }P_K\text{ is finite}).
\]
Prove $\beta_K=0$ for every $K\ge4$, or prove $\beta_K>0$ for some $K$ and determine its exact value. Nothing else is an answer. Return only when the existence and the exact value of $\beta_K$ are completely resolved and the argument has survived an adversarial audit. Do not return estimates, reductions, partial results, missing lemmas, computations, guesses, summaries, or discussions of difficulty. Never present an argument with a gap as complete.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Method (auxiliary polynomial across the $q$-adic places).} For each prime $q\mid M_p$ we have $q\equiv1\pmod{2p}$, and in $\mathbb{Z}_q$ there is a primitive $p$-th root of unity $\omega_q$ with $|2-\omega_q|_q=q^{-v_q(M_p)}$. Equivalently, $(2-\zeta_p)=\prod_{q\mid M_p}(q,\zeta_p-2)^{v_q(M_p)}$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_p]$, and $\prod_q|2-\omega_q|_q=M_p^{-1}$ (Liouville equality). Using Siegel's lemma, Laurent interpolation determinants, or the Bombieri--Pila and Heath-Brown determinant method, build $F\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ with controlled degree and height vanishing to high order at every $\omega_q$, $q\in S\subseteq\{q\mid M_p\}$. Evaluate at $x=2^j$ ($0\le j<p$) and compare the archimedean size with the product formula. The required gain must come from $|S|$, not from the approximation quality. Use $\tau(2^p+1)\le K$, that is, $(2+\zeta_p)$ has at most $\log_2(K/2)$ prime-ideal factors and $(2-\zeta_p)+(2+\zeta_p)=4$, wherever it helps. The argument must be unconditional, with explicit constants uniform in $p$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Audit (mandatory).} (i) Name the step that fails if every $\Phi_d(2)$ is replaced by a fake product of primes $\equiv1\pmod d$ of the right size that satisfies every bounded-depth congruence rule. Such models reach $\beta_K\ge\log2$, so an argument surviving them is void. (ii) For each cited theorem, check its hypotheses when degrees or numbers of places grow with $p$. (iii) Check consistency with the data: $\omega(M_p)\le5$ for $p\in P_4$, $p\le293$, and $\max_{d\le600}\omega(\Phi_d(2))=8$. (iv) Re-read the argument as a hostile referee.
